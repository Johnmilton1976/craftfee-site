REFUNDS · WHAT COMES BACK

The Etsy refund fee, line by line: what comes back and what doesn't

Refund an order and the fees do not all disappear with it. Some come back automatically, one comes back only if you take a second step, and two never come back at all. Knowing which is which is the difference between an annoying refund and an expensive one.

The short version

FeeRefund onlyRefund + cancel order
Transaction fee — 6.5%Comes back automaticallyComes back
Payment processing — 3% + fixedComes back automaticallyComes back
Listing fee — $0.20Does not come backCredited when the order is cancelled
Offsite Ads — 15% or 12%Never comes backNever comes back

Two of the biggest lines reverse themselves. The trap is the bottom half: the listing fee needs a second click most sellers never make, and Offsite Ads is gone no matter what you do.

Where the "automatic" part comes from. Etsy's own help centre, in "How to Cancel a Sale": "If you refund a buyer through your Shop Manager, your Etsy transaction and processing fees for the refunded transactions are automatically refunded to your Payment account." And for cancellations: "Etsy automatically credits you for the related listing and transaction fees." No form, no ticket, no 48-hour window.

A myth worth retiring: the "48-hour fee refund request"

Several blogs and even some fee calculators claim that Etsy keeps the 6.5% transaction fee on refunds unless you actively request it back within 48 hours. That advice comes from misreading Etsy's cases process — the formal dispute flow where a transaction is cancelled through Etsy's case system — and applying it to ordinary refunds.

For an ordinary refund you issue yourself through Shop Manager, the transaction fee and the processing fee return to your payment account automatically, with no deadline and no request. If you read elsewhere that you must "claim your fee back within 48 hours", check the date and the source against Etsy's current help pages — and then simply look at your own payment account after your next refund. The credit is there.

On real numbers: a $45 order, refunded

A US seller sells one item for $45, no shipping charged, no sales tax, single quantity. The buyer asks to return it, the seller agrees, and issues a full refund through Shop Manager:

LineChargedBack after refundBack after refund + cancel
Listing fee$0.20—$0.20
Transaction fee (6.5%)$2.93$2.93$2.93
Payment processing (3% + $0.25)$1.60$1.60$1.60
Total$4.73$4.53$0.00

Refund alone: you get $4.53 of the $4.73 back, and the twenty cents you donated is the listing fee. Cancel the order as well (the two-step Etsy recommends for cancelled sales) and the listing fee follows — zero Etsy fees remain on a sale that no longer exists.

But make it an Offsite Ads sale and the story changes. On the same $45 order, an Offsite Ads fee is $6.75 — more than all the standard fees combined. It does not come back on a refund, so the "free" refund just cost you $6.75 plus the listing fee. Offsite Ads is what turns a routine return into a real loss. The rule of thumb: a refunded organic sale costs you almost nothing in Etsy fees; a refunded Offsite Ads sale costs you the ad fee.

Where the money for the refund actually comes from

Etsy does not pay the buyer out of some fee pool — the refund is deducted from your payment account. If the balance there is not enough to cover it, Etsy charges the card on file for the difference. Two consequences sellers meet the hard way: a refund issued in a slow week lands on your credit card instead of quietly reducing a payout you were expecting, and a refund issued just after a big payout can push the account negative.

The buyer also chooses how the money comes back — original payment method or Etsy credit — and you cannot override that choice. Credit is instant; a card refund usually takes 3–5 business days to appear on the buyer's statement, counted from when the refund finishes processing. The refund itself can take up to 48 hours to complete on Etsy's side, so "I refunded it an hour ago" and "the buyer has their money" are different states, and telling a buyer which one you are in saves a support message.

The 180-day window — and what happens after it

Etsy Payments refunds are available from the moment the payment processes until 180 days after it. Past that line the refund option disappears: Etsy's help centre is explicit that after 180 days you cannot refund an order through Etsy Payments, and if your shop policy still promises one you would have to send it outside Etsy — PayPal, bank transfer — in which case Etsy cannot reverse your seller fees either.

That turns a goodwill refund into a full-cost one: you return 100% of the money and keep 0% of the fee reversal, plus the foreign exchange on the way back. If you sell anything with a long tail — custom work, made-to-order, a deposit or layaway arrangement — decide your refund window now and keep it inside 180 days, so the fee machinery is still working in your favour when you use it.

What a refund still costs you, even with every fee back

  • Postage you already paid. The label you bought through Etsy is a separate purchase. Refund any unused label separately, and eat the cost of the used one.
  • Return shipping. Unless your policy says otherwise, the trip back to you is on you or the buyer — and on international orders it can exceed the item's value.
  • Exchange rate movement. If the sale was converted between currencies, you absorb the spread in both directions; Etsy's conversion fee is not refunded.
  • The review. A cancelled or refunded order can still be reviewed within the eligibility window.
  • Time and materials. Packaging, labour and the day it spent in transit are gone. Fees coming back is not the same as the sale never having happened.

Partial refunds, worked out

The reversal follows the refunded amount: refund part of the order and the percentage fees charged on that part come back. On the same $45 order:

Amount refundedTransaction fee back (6.5%)Processing back (3%)Total backStill gone
$10$0.65$0.30$0.95$3.78
$20$1.30$0.60$1.90$2.83
$30$1.95$0.90$2.85$1.88
$45 — full refund$2.93$1.60$4.53$0.20

Read the last row against the others: a full refund is the only case where the $0.25 fixed processing charge is reliably part of the reversal, because it is charged once per transaction rather than as a percentage. Whether it comes back on a partial refund is not something Etsy's help pages promise either way — the honest answer is that your own payment account settles it, and one partial refund will tell you more than any blog post. What is certain: the listing fee never returns on a partial refund, no matter how much of the order you give back. Only a full refund plus cancellation takes the sale out of existence.

Refund or cancel — and in which order

They are two separate actions, and Etsy's own help page says so in as many words: "Be sure to cancel the order if needed. Canceling is separate from refunding." Both sit behind the same three-dot menu on the order, and Etsy's cancellation flow includes a single path that ticks Issue a full refund and Cancel order together — which is the one to use when the sale is genuinely dead.

  • Refund only. Money goes back, transaction and processing fees reverse, the listing fee stays gone.
  • Cancel (which refunds). Money goes back and Etsy credits the listing fee alongside the transaction fee. For an order that never shipped, this is the complete reversal.
  • Partial refund. For a discount after the fact, a damage settlement, or a return you do not want back. Cannot be combined with cancellation, because the order still exists.

Two timing limits catch sellers out. You cannot refund or cancel while the payment is still processing — most Etsy Payments orders take 24–72 hours to clear, and until they do the buttons are simply not there. And once an order is marked shipped, cancellation is no longer offered: from that point it is a refund plus a return, with the return shipping and the review risk that come with it. Decide before you print the label.

If the buyer opens a case instead of asking

A case is the buyer's escalation. Before opening one they are expected to contact you and wait — Etsy's process gives the seller roughly 48 hours to respond first, which is why the first message you send matters more than the tenth. Etsy then reviews the listing description, photos, message history and your shop policies; the paper trail you built in messages is the case file.

The practical arithmetic: a refund you issue yourself costs the fee gap described on this page. A case that goes against you costs the same money plus a mark on the shop's standing. If the buyer is right, refunding before the case is opened is the cheaper of the two — even on an Offsite Ads order where the ad fee is already lost.

Chargebacks are a different animal

A chargeback is the buyer disputing the charge with their card issuer instead of with you. Etsy responds to the card network on your behalf, but if the dispute goes against you, the amount is recovered from your account plus a dispute fee that runs roughly US$15–25 depending on region — chargeable regardless of how the underlying dispute resolves. This is why almost every chargeback is worth settling as a normal refund first: a refund costs you a listing fee; a lost chargeback costs you the money, the item and the fee.

Before you click refund

  • Did it ever ship? If not, cancel as well — that is what returns the listing fee.
  • Has the payment cleared? The refund and cancel buttons do nothing until it has.
  • Was it an Offsite Ads order? That fee is not coming back, so the refund is more expensive than the fee table suggests.
  • Who pays return shipping? On an international return it is usually the largest number in the whole transaction.
  • Is there an unused postage label? Labels are separate purchases; refund the label on its own or you eat it.
  • Is this inside 180 days? A goodwill promise made last year cannot be refunded through Etsy Payments at all.

Quick answers

Does Etsy refund its fees automatically?

The transaction fee and payment processing fee, yes — automatically, for the refunded amount, when you refund through Shop Manager. The listing fee follows only if you also cancel the order. The Offsite Ads fee never returns.

Should I refund, or cancel, or both?

If the order is truly dead — item returned, buyer made whole — do both: issue the refund through Shop Manager, then cancel the order from Orders & Delivery. Refund alone leaves the listing fee on the table; refund plus cancellation returns every standard fee.

Is there a fee for issuing a refund?

No. Etsy charges nothing for the act of refunding, and the buyer-facing refund equals what the buyer paid. What you "pay" is the slice of fees that does not reverse — listing fee unless you cancel, and Offsite Ads fee always.

What about the regulatory operating fee on a refund?

In the countries that have one — the regulatory fee applies to a minority of sellers — check how it appears on your own statement after a refund; Etsy's help pages are not explicit about it, and your payment account is the authority on what actually came back.

How long do I have to refund an order?

180 days from when the payment processed, through Etsy Payments. After that the button is gone, and a refund sent outside Etsy does not reverse your seller fees.

Does a partial refund return the $0.25 processing charge?

Treat it as no. The percentage portions reverse proportionally; the fixed per-transaction charge is not something Etsy's help pages promise back on partials. One refund will tell you what your own account does.

Where do I see the fees that came back?

Shop Manager → Finances → payment account, as separate lines against the refund, usually a day or two after the refund processes. The buyer's side can take 3–5 business days longer, which is why buyers often report that nothing happened yet.

How do I price so refunds don't hurt?

Start from your margin, not your cost: the fee calculator shows the profit left on any order, and the pricing guide shows the discount floor below which a return starts costing you money. A return policy you can afford is one priced in advance.

Behaviour described here follows Etsy's public help-centre pages ("How to Cancel a Sale", Fees & Payments Policy, Offsite Ads and Payments policies) as read in October 2026. Etsy can change its policies; your payment account statement is the final word on what was charged and what came back. This page is general information, not legal or financial advice.
