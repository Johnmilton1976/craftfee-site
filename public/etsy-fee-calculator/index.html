Which fees does Etsy actually charge in 2026?

Etsy charges a stack of separate fees, and — this is the part most calculators get wrong — they are not all calculated on the same amount.

FeeRateCharged on
Listing fee$0.20 per itemEvery listing, and every extra unit sold
Transaction fee6.5%Item + shipping + gift wrap (not tax)
Payment processing3% + $0.25 (US)Item + shipping + gift wrap + tax
Regulatory operating fee0 – 1.97%Item + shipping + gift wrap (some countries only)
Offsite Ads15% or 12%The whole order, capped at US$100 per order
Currency conversion2.5%Converted amount, only if the currencies differ
Two bases, not one. The transaction fee excludes sales tax; payment processing includes it. A calculator that applies 6.5% to the tax-inclusive total overstates your fees. One that leaves tax out of the processing base understates them. This calculator applies each rate to the right base.

What this Etsy fee calculator counts, and what it cannot

An Etsy fee calculator is only as honest as its list of inputs. This one counts the six charges Etsy actually takes — listing, transaction, payment processing, regulatory operating fee, Offsite Ads and currency conversion — plus sales tax and VAT where they change the base. Everything is applied to the base Etsy uses, not to a single convenient total.

What it cannot count is your own cost of doing business: materials, postage you buy, packaging, your time, and the return you will eventually have to absorb. Those are the inputs you type in yourself, and the difference between a fee calculator and a profit calculator is exactly that box marked your material cost. Sellers who skip it learn their real margin from a bank statement three months later.

It also cannot tell you which orders were attributed to Offsite Ads — only Etsy knows that, and only after the fact. The toggle above is a "what if" for pricing decisions, not a report of what already happened.

Two details almost every Etsy fee calculator misses

1. Processing is set by your bank account, not your buyer. Etsy charges the payment processing fee according to where your bank account sits — so a US shop pays 3% + $0.25 whether the buyer is in Ohio or Osaka.

2. But Canada and Australia have a second, higher rate for foreign orders. Canadian shops pay 3% + C$0.25 on orders from Canada or the US, and 4% + C$0.25 on orders from anywhere else. Australia works the same way: 3% + A$0.25 domestic, 4% + A$0.25 international. Switch on Buyer is outside my country above and the extra point appears in the fee breakdown.

Why do different Etsy fee calculators give different answers?

Three reasons. Sales tax handled incorrectly (see above). Regulatory fees that changed on 22 June 2026 — the UK moved from 0.32% to 0.48%, France from 0.47% to 1.14%, Italy from 0.32% to 0.80%, Spain from 0.72% to 0.88%, and many sites are still running the old numbers. And Offsite Ads applied to the item price rather than the order total.

What is a normal profit margin on Etsy?

For handmade and print-on-demand sellers, a healthy target is 30–50% net margin after all Etsy fees and product costs. Below 15%, a single refund, return or promoted-listing click can push an order underwater — that is the "selling at a loss without knowing it" zone. The rating beside the results uses these bands.

Should I price shipping into the item, or charge it separately?

The transaction fee applies to shipping you charge, so both approaches cost the same in Etsy fees. What differs is buyer psychology and Etsy search ranking, which favours free shipping. Put the number in the shipping field and compare.

Checking the calculator against your own Etsy statement

The useful test of any fee calculator is reconciliation: take one real order from Shop Manager → Finances, and compare line by line rather than comparing totals. Open the order and you should find a listing fee, a transaction fee, a payment processing fee, a regulatory fee if your country has one, and — on an attributed order only — an advertising charge. If the calculator's total is off by a few cents, it is almost always one of these four causes:

  • Rounding per line. Etsy rounds each fee to the cent before adding; a calculator that rounds only the total will differ by a cent or two.
  • The listing fee is charged in US dollars. Outside the US it is converted, so the amount on your statement moves with the exchange rate rather than sitting at a fixed local figure.
  • Tax on Etsy's own fees. If your country adds VAT or GST to the fees and you have not given Etsy a tax number, that surcharge is on the statement but easy to forget in a spreadsheet.
  • An Offsite Ads attribution you did not know about. A 15% line appears with no warning and no click you can trace; 30-day attribution means the ad was clicked weeks earlier.

If the gap is bigger than that, the rate has probably changed. Etsy's regulatory operating fee moved on 22 June 2026 and its processing table varies by country — check the current figures on the regulatory fee page before assuming the tool is wrong.

Does the Etsy fee calculator handle VAT and sales tax?

Yes, and the distinction matters more than the arithmetic. Sales tax collected from the buyer is not your revenue, but Etsy charges payment processing on the tax-inclusive amount — so it belongs in the processing base only, which is what the tax field does. VAT inside a European price is different again: the field strips it out of your revenue instead of treating the sticker price as income, and the separate toggle adds VAT to Etsy's own fees for unregistered sellers. Mixing the two up is the single most common reason a seller believes a calculator is "wrong".

Why is the total a few cents different from my Etsy statement?

Rounding, converted listing fees, tax on Etsy's fees, or an Offsite Ads attribution you did not know about — in that order of likelihood. Compare line by line rather than comparing totals, and the cause is usually obvious in seconds. If the difference is a percentage point or more, check whether your country's regulatory fee changed.
