AUSTRALIA · GST

Etsy fees for Australian sellers — and the GST that shows up twice

Australia is one of the cheapest developed markets to sell from on paper: two processing rates like Canada, but no regulatory operating fee on top. The complication is GST, which reaches you twice — on what your buyers pay, and on the fees Etsy charges you.

The Australian fee stack in 2026

FeeRateCharged on

GST on Etsy's fees10%Added to every fee unless Etsy has your GST ID or ABN

The shape is close to Canada's — same two-tier processing table, same 6.5% transaction fee — with one important difference: there is no regulatory operating fee. On identical orders an Australian shop keeps roughly half a percentage point more than a Canadian one.

The split is domestic versus everywhere else. A buyer in Sydney pays the 3% rate. A buyer in Ohio, London or Auckland pays 4% + A$0.25 — a full percentage point more. Unlike Canada, there is no US exception: an American buyer is an international buyer for an Australian shop.

Domestic versus international, on real numbers

The same A$30 item with free shipping, no sales tax, single quantity, no advertising:

Buyer in AustraliaA$0.29A$1.95A$1.15A$3.39A$26.61
Buyer anywhere elseA$0.29A$1.95A$1.45A$3.69A$26.31

Thirty cents on an A$30 order — the effective rate moves from 11.3% to 12.3% purely on where the buyer lives. Most Australian Etsy shops sell mainly to the United States and Europe, so plan your pricing around the 4% line, not the 3% one. (For comparison: a Canadian seller pays 11.8% and 12.8% on the same orders, because Canada stacks a 0.50% regulatory fee on top.)

That is not a discount — it is the flat charges spreading thinner over a bigger number, and it explains most of the "why do small orders feel unprofitable" questions in Australian seller forums.

Why cheap items are expensive here

On a A$10 order the two flat charges — A$0.29 listing and A$0.25 fixed processing — take 5.4% before a single percentage fee applies. Add 6.5% transaction and 4% overseas processing and the stack reaches 15.9%; add GST on the fees and it is 17.5%. The same shop selling at A$100 pays 11.8%. Nothing changed about the item, the buyer or the postage: flat fees simply do not scale down.

Three ways out, roughly in order of how well they work. Raise the average order value — a second item, a gift box, a bundle. Sell sets instead of singles, so the same goods move as one unit and one listing fee covers the lot rather than one per piece. And set a price floor somewhere around A$20–25, below which an order cannot carry its own overhead. A A$10 sticker price is not a cheap item; it is an item whose fee percentage roughly doubles.

A fuller example, with shipping

Sell one item for A$35 with A$7 shipping to a buyer in Melbourne, so the order total is A$42. No sales tax, no advertising:

Etsy takes 10.8% of the order — and note the shipping was not exempt from any of it: the A$7 sat inside the base for both the transaction fee and the processing fee.

Then add GST on the fees themselves. If Etsy does not have your GST ID or ABN on file, it adds 10% GST to every fee it charges an Australian seller: on this order about A$0.45, bringing the total to roughly A$4.98. Submit your GST ID in Shop Manager → Finances → Payment settings and the GST line disappears — registered businesses are invoiced under reverse-charge rules instead and account for their own GST.
Now make it an Offsite Ads order. If the buyer arrived through an Etsy ad, the same A$42 order carries an extra 15%: A$6.30. Total fees jump to A$10.83 and you keep A$31.17. The effective rate goes from 10.8% to 25.8% — one dollar in four. See the Offsite Ads page for when you can opt out.

GST: the two places it touches you

1. GST on sales to Australian buyers. Since 1 July 2019, Etsy automatically collects 10% GST at checkout on physical goods valued at A$1,000 or less that are imported into Australia, and remits it to the ATO. The tax is never your revenue — it passes through Etsy — but the payment processing fee still applies to the tax-inclusive amount, which is why processing on a taxed order looks higher than expected. Orders shipped within Australia, and consignments above A$1,000, are outside this automatic collection.

2. GST on Etsy's fees. Without a GST ID on file, every fee in the table above carries an extra 10%. With one on file, Etsy invoices the fees GST-free and you handle GST on your side of the relationship. Registration is only mandatory once your turnover passes A$75,000, but the GST-on-fees line starts from your very first sale — which is one more reason to hand Etsy your ABN early if you have one.

Pricing to a margin, in Australian dollars

Because the 4% rate applies to the overseas buyers most Australian shops depend on, price against that line rather than the domestic one. With GST added to Etsy's fees, the percentage fees total 11.55% (10.5% × 1.1) and the flat charges A$0.59 (A$0.54 × 1.1), which gives the price for any target margin:

Price = (cost + 0.59) ÷ (1 − 0.1155 − target margin)
With a GST ID or ABN on file, Etsy's fees carry no GST: Price = (cost + 0.54) ÷ (1 − 0.105 − target margin).
Using the domestic 3% rate instead would let you shave about one percent off the price — and misprice every overseas order.

What changes if you are GST-registered

  • Etsy stops adding GST to its fees. With a GST ID or ABN on file, fees are invoiced GST-free and you account for GST yourself under reverse-charge rules. Every figure in the tables above drops by about a point — the A$30 order goes from 13.5% to 12.3%, the A$42 example from A$4.98 back to A$4.53.
  • You charge GST on Australian sales yourself. Registered sellers add 10% to domestic sales; the automatic collection Etsy performs covers goods imported into Australia up to A$1,000, which is a different mechanism — and not your revenue either way.
  • You claim GST credits on business inputs — materials, packaging, postage, the Etsy fees themselves — through your BAS.
  • Registration is only compulsory above A$75,000 of turnover, but the GST-on-fees line starts from your first sale. Handing Etsy your ABN early is usually worth more than waiting for the threshold.

Quick answers

Why does a cheap order carry such a high fee percentage?

Flat charges. A$0.29 listing plus A$0.25 fixed processing is 5.4% of a A$10 order and 0.27% of a A$200 one. The percentage fees are identical at every price; the flats are not, so they hurt cheapest items most.

Should I price for the 3% or the 4% processing rate?

The 4%. Most Australian shops sell predominantly overseas, and a buyer in the United States counts as international here — unlike Canada, there is no US exception.

Is there really no regulatory operating fee in Australia?

Really no. As of late 2026 Etsy charges it only to shops with bank accounts in countries including the UK (0.48%), Canada (0.50%), France (1.14%), Italy (0.80%), Spain (0.88%), Hungary, India, Türkiye and Vietnam. Australia is absent from the list — one of the structural reasons Australian shops keep slightly more than British or Canadian ones on identical sales.

Does the listing fee change with quantity?

Yes. Sell three units in one order and you pay three listing fees — about A$0.87 rather than A$0.29. It is the most commonly missed line on low-price, high-volume listings.

What is the Offsite Ads cap in Australian dollars?

US$100, which works out to roughly A$140 depending on the exchange rate. Etsy sets the cap in US dollars, so the figure in your own currency moves.

Should I list in AUD or USD?

List in the currency your buyers use, and let your bank account be AUD. If your listing currency differs from your payout currency, Etsy takes 2.5% of every sale in conversion fees — on an A$30 order that is A$0.75, more than double the listing fee.

How do I check my own numbers?

Use the fee calculator, select Australia, and switch on Buyer is outside my country for overseas orders — it applies the 4% + A$0.25 rate automatically. If you refund orders, read which fees come back on a refund.
