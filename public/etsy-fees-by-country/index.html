Etsy fees by country in 2026 — Canada, Australia and Germany

These three are the interesting cases outside the UK and the EU. Canada and Australia share a two-rate payment table that most calculators flatten to one number. Germany has no regulatory fee but the steepest VAT-on-fees line of the three. Here is each, with the numbers that change by where your buyer lives.

The three side by side

Two things separate them. Canada and Australia have a second, higher processing rate for foreign buyers — a full percentage point more on every order from abroad. And only Canada pays a regulatory operating fee; Australia and Germany pay none. The tax on Etsy's own fees varies from 5% to 19% and, for an unregistered seller, is a real cost on top of everything else.

Canada — the two-rate market with a regulatory fee

Canada is one of only two markets with two payment processing rates, and it is the only one of these three with a regulatory operating fee (0.50%). The split is Canada-and-the-US versus the rest of the world:

Most Canadian shops sell mostly to Americans, who count as domestic — so a US sale is actually cheaper to serve than a British or Australian one. GST or HST lands on Etsy's fees at 5% (Alberta) to 15% (Atlantic provinces); registered sellers reclaim it, unregistered sellers absorb it. Use the calculator and switch on Buyer is outside my country for the overseas rate.

Australia — two rates, but no regulatory fee

Australia mirrors Canada's two-tier processing table but adds no regulatory fee, so on identical orders an Australian shop keeps about half a point more than a Canadian one. The difference from Canada: there is no US exception — a buyer in Ohio is international for an Australian shop.

GST reaches an Australian seller twice: Etsy collects 10% on imported goods up to A$1,000 at checkout, and it adds 10% GST to its own fees unless you have given it a GST ID or ABN. Without one, that 10% is a permanent cost from your first sale — which is why handing Etsy your ABN early is usually worth it.

Germany — one rate, no regulatory fee, steep VAT on fees

Germany has the ordinary European processing rate and no regulatory fee, so its fee bill looks low — until the 19% VAT on Etsy's own fees. That line is what most calculators miss:

Order (€100, no ads)Fees, no VAT IDFees, VAT ID on file

Under the Kleinunternehmerregelung (§19 UStG) you do not charge VAT on sales and cannot deduct input VAT — which includes the 19% Etsy charges on its own fees. So for a small German shop that VAT is a permanent cost, not a timing difference. Submit a USt-IdNr. and Etsy stops charging it from the following month.

Quick answers

Why does a cheap order carry such a high fee percentage?

Flat charges. In every currency the listing fee plus the fixed processing charge is a fixed toll that never scales down — about 5% of a 10-unit order and under 0.3% of a 200-unit one.

Should I price for the 3% or the 4% processing rate?

The 4% — the overseas rate — is the one most Canadian and Australian shops actually earn, because most of their buyers are abroad. Using the domestic 3% underprices every foreign order.

Do these three countries have a regulatory operating fee?

Only Canada (0.50%). Australia and Germany are not on Etsy's published list, so they pay none — one reason their fee bills look lighter than the UK's.

How do I check my own numbers?

Use the fee calculator, pick your country, and switch on the toggles that apply — Buyer is outside my country for Canada and Australia, and VAT/GST on Etsy's fees where relevant. The VAT on fees and regulatory fee pages track the rates Etsy reset on 22 June 2026.
