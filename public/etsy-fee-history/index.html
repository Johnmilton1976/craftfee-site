FEE HISTORY

Etsy's fee history: two rate rises, six changes, one $30 order

The headline transaction rate moved twice in nine years. Your total cost moved six times. Run the same order through every era and the arithmetic explains why the spreadsheet you built in 2017 is still costing you money.

Short version. A US$30 order (a $25 item plus $5 shipping) cost a US seller $2.23 in fees in 2017 and costs $3.30 today — 7.4% of the order becoming 11.0%. Half of that happened on two dates everyone remembers. The other half arrived as base changes and add-on fees that were never marketed as an increase.

The same $30 order, in every era

Take one order: a $25 item, $5 shipping, no gift wrap, no ads, a US bank account, no state tax on fees. Nothing about this order changes across the four columns below. Only the rules do.

For a US seller, nothing has been added at the per-order level since April 2022. That is worth stating plainly, because the common claim that Etsy keeps raising its cut is not true of the transaction rate — it is true of everything around it. Where the cost has kept climbing since 2022 is in the add-ons: the setup fee for new shops, the instant transfer fee, sales tax on fees in two states, and a regulatory fee that has now been reset twice.

The other number worth holding onto: $1.07 more per order than 2017, which is 3.9% of your take-home at this price. A shop that has not raised prices since 2018 has absorbed that out of margin.

The 2018 increase had two halves, and the quiet one was 40%

This is the part most write-ups miss. On 16 July 2018 Etsy did two things at once: it moved the rate from 3.5% to 5%, and it moved the base from the item price to the order total. Before that date shipping was not charged the transaction fee at all.

Transaction fee on a $25 item + $5 shippingRateBaseFeeChange
Before 16 Jul 20183.5%$25 item only$0.88—
Rate rise only (counterfactual)5%$25 item only$1.25+$0.38
What actually happened5%$30 item + shipping$1.50+$0.63

Split that $0.63 apart. The rate rise accounts for $0.375. The base change accounts for $0.25 — 40% of the increase, delivered by a change that was never described as a fee rise. The headline rate went up 43%; the fee line on this order went up 71%.

The practical consequence is the one that still bites: any pricing sheet built before July 2018 is wrong twice. The rate is too low and the base is too small. If you price a $25 item with $5 shipping off 3.5%-of-item math, you will undercount this fee line by $0.62 per sale even before the 2022 rise.

What the add-ons do to that same order

Two of those add-ons are small enough to be ignored and shouldn't be. Sales tax on Etsy's own fees, for a Texas seller at the 8.25% local maximum, adds $0.27 to this order — about 0.9 points on the rate, for a fee that Etsy did not raise. And instant transfer is capped at $1.00 per transfer with one transfer allowed per day, which means the ceiling on that fee is $365 a year — real money if you use it daily for cash flow, irrelevant if you withdraw once a month.

The one to actually watch is Offsite Ads, because it is the only add-on that can more than double your effective rate on a single order. It is also the one with a switch: below $10,000 in trailing 12-month revenue it is optional. The mechanics are on the Offsite Ads page, including the 30-day attribution window that catches orders you thought were organic.

To keep your 2017 take-home, charge 4% more

The useful way to read nine years of changes is not the rate but the price you would need today. Solve for the price that leaves you the same $27.78 a $30 order left you in 2017, at current US rates:

You keep = price − $0.20 − 6.5% of price − (3% of price + $0.25)
You keep = 0.905 × price − $0.45
0.905 × price − $0.45 = $27.78 → price = $31.19

A $30 item in 2017 has to be $31.19 today to hand you the same money — a 4.0% price rise. That is the whole history in one line: not a 3-point fee increase, a 4% price increase. If you have not repriced since 2018, the gap is wider still, because the 2018 change cost you 3.9% of take-home on its own.

Exactly three points at every price, because the change was purely the transaction rate moving from 3.5% to 6.5%. What varies with price is not the size of the increase but the weight of the two flat fees. At $10 the $0.20 listing fee and the $0.25 processing fixed fee are 4.5 points of your total on their own; at $250 they are 0.2 points. That is why pricing low on Etsy fails for a different reason than most sellers think — not because 6.5% is high, but because 45 cents never shrinks.

How the increase looked in practice

The 11 April 2022 change produced the largest seller protest in Etsy's history: shops went on vacation mode during that week and a petition against the rise gathered tens of thousands of signatures. The rate stayed, and it is still 6.5% today. If you want to see how that fee behaves when an order is refunded rather than completed, the refund page breaks it down line by line, including the part that does not come back.

The regulatory fee has its own timeline

The regulatory operating fee behaves differently from everything else here: it is a percentage that exists only in some countries, it uses the same base as the transaction fee, and it is reset rather than announced as a general increase. The UK is the clearest example, having been reset twice.

Three of the nine went down or stayed put in the same update. That is worth noticing: the fee is reviewed yearly and it is not a one-way ratchet — India's fell by 83%. It is also the one fee most sellers never see, because it appears as a single line in the payment account with no explanation.

What you cannot reverse

The transaction fee, the payment processing rate, the listing fee and the 2018 base change are not negotiable and have no setting. Neither is the regulatory operating fee — a VAT ID does not remove it, because it is not a tax on your sales but a surcharge covering Etsy's own compliance costs. The only response to those is price, which is why the $31.19 above is the number that matters more than the 11.0%.

Questions people ask about Etsy's fee history

When did Etsy last raise its transaction fee?

11 April 2022, from 5% to 6.5%. It has not moved since. The previous rise was 16 July 2018, from 3.5% to 5%, and that one also widened the base to include shipping for the first time.

Has Etsy ever raised fees without changing the headline rate?

Yes, repeatedly. The 2018 change was half rate and half base, and the base produced 40% of the increase. Since then: a one-time setup fee in September 2024, a 1% instant transfer fee and sales tax on Etsy's own fees in December 2025, and the June 2026 regulatory reset.

How much more does Etsy take now than in 2017?

On the same $30 order, $2.23 (7.4%) in 2017 against $3.30 (11.0%) today for a US seller with no ads — $1.07 more per order, straight out of margin.

Did Etsy reverse the 2022 increase after the seller protest?

No. Shops went on vacation mode during the week of 11 April 2022 and a petition gathered tens of thousands of signatures. The 6.5% rate stayed and is still in force.

What is the newest Etsy fee in 2026?

The regulatory operating fee reset on 22 June 2026 — introduced in Hungary at 1.97%, and raised sharply in France and Italy. The newest fee overall is the 1% instant transfer fee added to the Payments Policy effective 12 December 2025.

Every rate on this page was checked on 2 November 2026. Fee calculations are shown in US dollars for a US seller unless a country is named, and they exclude sales tax on the goods themselves, which the buyer pays. Confirm current terms in Etsy's Fees & Payments Policy before you price anything.
