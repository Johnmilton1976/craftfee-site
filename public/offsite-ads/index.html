OFFSITE ADS

Etsy Offsite Ads fee — the 15%, the $100 cap, opting out

Etsy Offsite Ads promotes your listings on Google, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest and partner sites. You pay nothing upfront. But when a sale arrives through one of those ads, Etsy takes a further slice of the whole order — and that slice is bigger than any other fee on the invoice.

The short version. Etsy Offsite Ads takes 15% of the whole order — item, shipping and gift wrap — or 12% once your shop has reached US$10,000 in any 365-day period. It is capped at US$100 per attributed order, charged only when a click becomes a sale, and a click stays attributable for 30 days. You can opt out only while the shop has always stayed under US$10,000; above that it is mandatory for the life of the shop.

The base is the whole order, not the item. This is the detail most fee calculators get wrong. Charge £5 shipping on a £30 item and Offsite Ads is charged on £35, not £30. Folding shipping into the item price does not escape it either.

12% vs 15%: which rate you pay, and when

Which Etsy Offsite Ads rate you pay comes down to one number — not your category, not your country, not how long you have been selling, but your shop's sales in any consecutive 365-day period.

Your shop's sales, any consecutive 365 daysFee on an attributed orderCan you opt out
Always under US$10,00015%Yes — and you can switch back on
US$10,000 or more, at any point12%No — required for the life of the shop

Three details get lost in the one-line summaries, and all three change the decision:

  • The window rolls; it does not reset on 1 January. "Any consecutive 365 days" means Etsy is asking whether your shop has ever had a year that good. A shop that peaks in November and then goes quiet does not drop back to 15% the following January — the threshold has already been crossed once, and once is enough.
  • It is measured in US dollars, converting each sale at the exchange rate Etsy used on the day it was processed. For sellers outside the US, the real threshold is a moving local figure — roughly £8,000, €9,000, C$14,000 or A$15,000 depending on the month. A weak pound moves the line closer without you selling anything extra. Country-by-country fee pages →
  • Crossing is permanent. Sales can fall back below US$10,000 afterwards; the 12% rate and the mandatory status stay for the lifetime of the shop anyway.
So the 12% rate is a discount and a closing door at the same time. Under the threshold you pay more per attributed order but you can switch the programme off. Over it you pay less and cannot leave. That trade — not the three points — is the actual decision, and it is made once.

On the same order, the two rates differ by exactly three percentage points of the order total:

Look at the last two rows. Because the $100 ceiling binds the 15% rate from about $667 and the 12% rate from about $833, the discount disappears entirely above $833 — both rates pay the same $100. If you sell high-ticket pieces, the 12% rate is worth almost nothing on your best orders and a great deal on your cheapest. The benefit lands where it is least needed, which is the opposite of what "12% instead of 15%" sounds like.

Both percentages are unchanged in 2026: 15% under the threshold, 12% above it, with no announced change to either. Etsy sets them in its Fees & Payments Policy, and every figure on this page was checked against that policy in October 2026.

The same product, the same price, and Etsy takes more than twice as much. That is where the widely repeated claim that "Etsy takes a third" comes from — it does, on Offsite Ads orders, once you add your material cost.

The $100 cap, and when it matters

The Etsy Offsite Ads fee can never exceed $100 per attributed order. For a $38 item that never binds. It starts to matter above roughly $667 at the 15% rate, or $833 at 12%.

This has a counter-intuitive consequence: on high-value orders, Offsite Ads becomes cheaper as a percentage. A $2,000 order pays $100, not $300. If you sell expensive pieces, an Offsite Ads order is often better than its headline rate suggests — while a $50 order is worse.

Practical upshot. If your catalogue runs from cheap to expensive, the cheap items are the ones that get hurt by Offsite Ads. Consider whether your low-priced listings are worth having at all.

What 15% works out to per click

The fee is quoted as a percentage, which makes it hard to compare with any other advertising. Convert it. If the fee per order is 15% of the order value, and it takes 1 ÷ conversion rate clicks to produce one order, then the fee is economically a cost per click:

Implied cost per click = order value × 15% × conversion rate
A $38 order at 2% conversion implies $5.70 × 0.02 = $0.11 per click.

Read against Etsy Ads — where clicks commonly cost somewhere between $0.20 and $1.00 depending on category — the numbers explain something sellers often find counter-intuitive: Offsite Ads is frequently the cheaper of the two per click, sometimes several times cheaper. The reason is structural rather than generous. You pay nothing for clicks that do not convert; the ad budget is effectively shouldered by your successful orders. The catch is the flip side of the same design: you cannot cap it. There is no daily budget, no pause button, and no way to say "this month I would rather not".

Is it still profitable? A margin test

The only question that matters is what is left after the fee. Same $38 item, US seller, no shipping, comparing an organic sale with an attributed one:

The fee costs about 15 percentage points of margin at every level, because it is charged on revenue rather than profit. That is the test: if your gross margin before the fee is under about 20%, an attributed order is close to working for free, and the honest answer is that the product is priced too low for the channel — not that the ads are unfair. At 40%+ the order is still worth having, which is why the same programme is a problem for one shop and a gift for another.

If your shop has crossed $10,000 and pays the 12% rate instead, the same order carries $4.56 rather than $5.70 — three points of margin back, and the reason the mandatory rate is less painful than the headline suggests. Put your own order through the profit calculator at both rates before you decide it matters.

Can you opt out?

Opting out of Etsy Offsite Ads depends entirely on your sales history, measured over any consecutive 365-day period:

Shop revenue, any 365-day periodStatusRate
Always under $10,000Automatic enrolment, opt-out allowed15%
$10,000 or moreMandatory for the life of the shop12%

To opt out: Shop Manager → Settings → Offsite Ads → Opt out.

Whether you should opt out is a real decision, not an obvious one. The fee is painful, but the traffic is incremental — organic Etsy search may not replace those orders at all. The honest test is: are Offsite Ads orders profitable after the 15%? Run your typical order through the calculator with the toggle on. If it is still profitable, the ads are doing their job. If it is not, opt out and rebuild your pricing.

Offsite Ads is not Etsy Ads

Etsy Offsite Ads and Etsy Ads are two different products and sellers confuse them constantly.

Where ads appearGoogle, Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, partner sitesInside Etsy search and category pages
How you payPercentage of the order, only on a saleCost per click, whether or not you sell

Can you opt outOnly under $10,000 in salesJust switch the budget off

Attribution, and one more rule

A sale is attributed to Offsite Ads if the buyer clicked an ad and then bought from your shop within 30 days. One click can produce several attributed orders. If a buyer clicked an Offsite Ad but their last click before purchase was an Etsy on-site ad, then only the on-site charge applies — the two never stack on the same order.

You cannot choose which listings get promoted, and you cannot set a spending cap other than the $100 per order limit.

What to do about it

  • Price with it in mind. If a meaningful share of your orders come from Offsite Ads, your prices need to work at the 15% rate, not just the organic one.
  • Compare your actual rate. Etsy reports Offsite Ads performance in your shop stats. Check what share of your orders are attributed before deciding anything.
  • Decide deliberately. If you are under $10,000, opting in or out is a genuine choice. Run the numbers instead of reacting to the invoice.

Switch the Offsite Ads toggle on in the fee calculator to see the effect on your own prices — including the 15% and 12% variants.

How to tell whether an order was attributed

The fee shows up as its own line in the order's fee breakdown and in your payment account, separate from the listing, transaction and processing lines. If a sale shows only the usual charges, it was organic — whatever route the buyer actually took to find you. Your shop stats also report how many orders came through the programme and what it cost in total; that figure, as a share of revenue rather than a share of orders, is the one to check before making any decision.

Re-enabling, and what happens at $10,000

While you are under the threshold, opting out is reversible from the same settings screen: switch back in and subsequent attributed orders are charged again. Once a shop has reached US$10,000 in any consecutive 365-day period, participation becomes a condition of selling and the rate for the life of the shop becomes 12%. Note that the threshold is measured on sales, not profit — a shop that grows is quietly moved from "optional at 15%" to "mandatory at 12%".

Quick answers

How much is the Etsy Offsite Ads fee?

15% of the order total for shops that have always stayed under US$10,000 in sales, 12% once that threshold has been reached and participation is mandatory. Capped at US$100 per order.

Is the fee charged on shipping and gift wrap?

Yes. The base is the whole order total, not the item price. Folding shipping into the item price does not avoid it — the percentage simply applies to a larger number.

Do I still pay if the buyer returns the order?

Yes, and this is the expensive case. The Offsite Ads fee is not reversed when an order is refunded, so a returned attributed order costs you the fee with none of the revenue — see which fees come back on a refund.

Can I choose which listings get advertised?

No. Etsy selects the listings it promotes and you cannot nominate or exclude individual items, set a daily budget, or cap the spend beyond the per-order ceiling.

Is the Offsite Ads rate 12% or 15% in 2026?

Both, depending on the shop. 15% if your shop has always stayed under US$10,000 in sales across any consecutive 365-day period; 12% once it has reached that figure, where participation is mandatory. Nothing in the 2026 fee schedule changes either percentage, and both are capped at US$100 per order.

Does the $10,000 threshold reset each year?

No. It is any consecutive 365-day window rather than a calendar year, and crossing it once is permanent: the 12% rate and the mandatory enrolment stay for the life of the shop even if sales later fall below US$10,000. Before crossing, opting out is a genuine choice; afterwards it is not.

Rates and rules last verified October 2026. Etsy can change the Offsite Ads programme; always confirm current terms in Etsy's Advertising & Marketing Policy.
