ETSY SELLER TAX, EXPLAINED

Etsy seller tax: income tax, the 1099-K and what you actually owe

"Etsy tax" usually means one of two different jobs. The buyer-side tax, Etsy mostly handles. Your income tax — the tax on what you earn — Etsy does not touch: no withholding, no filing, no reminder. This page is only about that second job.

The 30-second version

Etsy collects and remits most buyer-side tax automatically — US state sales tax under marketplace facilitator laws, VAT on UK/EU digital orders, GST/HST in Canada and Australia. You will see it pass through your payment account; it is not your liability to file. The rates and the few exceptions are described on our VAT on fees and fees by country pages, so they are not repeated here. Everything below is about the money you owe: income tax on your profit, the self-employment tax, the 1099-K, and the quarterly deadlines that arrive whether or not you were ready.

Is your Etsy money taxable income?

Yes, from the very first sale. The IRS does not have a "small seller" exemption — hobby or business, $40 or $4,000, gross receipts are reportable income. Three distinctions decide what that means in practice:

  • Gross sales are not your taxable amount. The shipping you charge is part of gross receipts too — a line sellers forget. What you owe tax on is profit: gross sales minus fees, postage, materials and other ordinary expenses of the shop.
  • Fees are deductible. The whole bill — the $0.20 listing fee, the 6.5% transaction fee, the 15% Offsite Ads fee, payment processing — every one of them reduces taxable profit. The fee calculator totals that bill; the CSV Etsy lets you download is the evidence.
  • Refunded orders still count until you net them out. A refunded sale is not income once the refund is processed, but the original sale and the refund both need to appear in your records — netting them silently is what makes a return not match the 1099-K.

The two taxes on your profit

In the US, self-employed sellers owe federal income tax (plus state income tax where it applies) on profit, and separately the 15.3% self-employment tax on net earnings. The SE tax is Social Security and Medicare — the share an employer would normally split with you, which as a self-employed seller you pay whole. The calculation runs on 92.35% of net profit, not the full amount:

Net profit for the yearSelf-employment tax (15.3% × 92.35%)Set-aside at 28% of profit
$5,000$706$1,400
$10,000$1,413$2,800
$25,000$3,532$7,000
$50,000$7,065$14,000

The set-aside column is the practical answer to "how much should I keep back": 25 to 30 percent of profit covers federal income tax, state tax where it applies, and the SE tax for most mid-bracket sellers. The exact federal rate depends on your total household income and filing status, which is why an accountant who knows your state can tighten a number this page can only bracket.

Half of the self-employment tax you pay is deductible from your income tax calculation. It does not make the tax cheap — it stops you from paying income tax on money that was already spent on SE tax.

The 1099-K, plainly

If you sell through Etsy Payments in the US, Etsy issues a Form 1099-K when your gross sales pass the reporting threshold. That threshold has been a moving target — and if you have read that it drops to $600 in 2026, that is out of date:

Tax yearFederal 1099-K thresholdWhy
2024More than $5,000IRS transition relief (Notice 2024-85) during the phase-down
2025$2,500 planned — never took effectThe One Big Beautiful Bill (P.L. 119-21, July 2025) repealed the phase-down retroactively
2026 and afterMore than $20,000 AND more than 200 transactionsPre-ARPA rule restored — both conditions must be met, neither one alone is enough

Most Etsy sellers will therefore not receive a federal 1099-K. A seller with $9,000 in gross sales across 300 orders in 2026 clears the transaction count but not the dollar threshold, so no form arrives. The confusion is widespread because the $600 figure was law for four years and a lot of published guides still print it.

Several states set their own reporting thresholds, some at $600 all along, so a seller can still receive a state-level 1099-K. And the rule that matters most survives every rewrite of the threshold: a 1099-K is an information return. Receiving one is not an audit flag; not reporting income that appears on one is. No 1099-K is not a tax exemption either — your profit is taxable whether or not a form exists.

A 1099-K reports your gross transactions to the IRS — it is not a bill for tax on that whole amount. You owe income tax on your profit. This is the single most common 1099-K panic: the form says $9,000, the seller assumes they owe tax on $9,000, when the taxable figure after fees and materials might be a third of that. Reconcile the form against your CSV records before you file, and keep both.

Verified 4 November 2026 against three IRS sources: the Form 1099-K FAQs (Fact Sheet 2025-08, updated 23 Oct 2025), the Instructions for Form 1099-K (December 2026 revision) and the IRS "Get ready to file your taxes" guidance. All three state the restored rule — reporting is required only above $20,000 in gross payments and more than 200 transactions, retroactive to 2025.

Quarterly estimated taxes: the deadlines that actually bite

Employees pay tax through their paycheck as they earn. Self-employed sellers are expected to do the same, four times a year, once you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax for the year. The deadlines are not "quarterly" in any calendar sense — they are genuinely uneven, and the January one lands after the holidays when shop money is tightest:

Income periodEstimated tax deadline
January – March15 April
April – May15 June
June – August15 September
September – December15 January (next year)

You avoid the underpayment penalty through either of two routes, called safe harbours: pay 90% of this year's total tax across the four payments, or 100% of last year's total tax (110% if your adjusted gross income passed $150,000). The second route is the one that matters in a good year: even if your Etsy profit doubles, paying last year's amount on time costs you nothing extra in penalties, and the difference gets settled in April.

What is deductible, in plain categories

  • Etsy's charges: listing, transaction, payment processing, Offsite Ads, regulatory and listing-variation fees — the entire fee bill.
  • Postage and packaging: the shipping you charge is income, the postage you buy is an expense — they net against each other.
  • Materials and cost of goods: yarn, clay, canvas, resale inventory, anything that physically becomes the product.
  • Equipment and software: a cutting machine, a lightbox, design software — larger items may be depreciated or expensed in year one under the de minimis rules; an accountant picks the route.
  • The workspace: the home-office deduction if a space is used regularly and exclusively for the shop.

None of these need receipts attached to the return, but all of them need to survive a question later, which is why the monthly CSV export plus a folder of receipts is the whole system.

Selling outside the US

Income tax follows where you live, not where Etsy is incorporated — UK, EU, Canadian and Australian sellers pay income tax on Etsy profit under their own national rules, and none of it is withheld by Etsy either. VAT and GST registration is a separate question from income tax: charging and remitting VAT does not reduce your income tax, and the two get confused constantly. The country pages — UK, EU, Canada, Australia — cover the buyer-side and registration rules per market.

What to actually set up

  • Add your tax ID (SSN/EIN or the local equivalent) under Shop Manager → Finances → Legal and financial information.
  • Download the monthly CSV and keep it — it itemises sales, fees and tax collected, and it is what you will reconcile the 1099-K against.
  • Open a separate account for the shop's payouts and move the set-aside percentage out of it on the day each payout lands — before you spend it, not after.
  • Calendar the four estimated-tax deadlines with a one-week lead, since the payment itself takes a few minutes and the penalty for a missed quarter is computed per quarter.
  • Keep a one-folder record of deductible expenses as they happen; reconstructing a year of receipts in March is how deductions get abandoned.
Where these numbers come from. Etsy's own Taxes 101 seller handbook covers what Etsy collects, and the IRS Gig Economy Tax Center, the Form 1099-K page and the estimated taxes guide cover what you owe. This page is general information, not tax advice — thresholds and state rules change, so verify against those sources or ask an accountant.
