ETSY TAX, EXPLAINED

Etsy tax: what Etsy collects for you — and what it never will

The single most confused word in Etsy selling is "tax", because it covers two completely different things: the tax on what your buyers pay, and the tax on what Etsy charges you. Etsy handles most of the first kind and none of the second kind.

The line that matters

Every tax question about Etsy sorts into two buckets:

  • Tax on your sales — sales tax, VAT and GST added to what the buyer pays. In most cases Etsy calculates it, collects it from the buyer, and remits it to the government for you.
  • Tax on your profit — income tax. Etsy does nothing here. No withholding, no filing, no reminders. It is between you and your tax authority.

And there is a third, smaller bucket that surprises people: tax on Etsy's fees, which you pay as part of your fee bill. That one has its own page — VAT on Etsy fees — because the rules there differ from everything below.

What Etsy collects and remits for you

US state sales tax

Most US states have marketplace facilitator laws: when a platform like Etsy facilitates the sale, the platform — not you — must collect and remit the state sales tax. In practice, on orders shipped to addresses in most states, Etsy adds the right sales tax at checkout, takes it from the buyer, and pays the state. You cannot opt out, and Etsy charges no extra administrative fee for handling it.

  • You will see it in your payment account as sales tax collected and remitted — it passes through, it is not deducted from your earnings.
  • Digital downloads are covered too: Etsy handles sales tax on downloadable orders in many states.
  • For the few places Etsy does not cover automatically, you can still set your own rates under Shop Manager → Finances → Payment settings → Sales tax, and you file those yourself.
  • Colorado adds a 28-cent retail delivery fee on physical orders shipped in-state — Etsy collects it from the buyer and remits it, so do not be surprised by the line item.

VAT and GST on orders outside the US

Outside the US the same idea applies under local names. For UK and EU buyers, Etsy collects VAT at checkout on digital items and on many low-value physical imports. For Canadian buyers, Etsy has collected GST/HST on eligible orders since July 2022, plus QST in Quebec and provincial sales taxes in British Columbia, Saskatchewan and Manitoba. In Australia and New Zealand, GST is collected on eligible orders the same way.

One Canadian exception trips people up: if you have added a GST/HST ID to your Etsy account, Etsy stops collecting on your physical sales — you are expected to include the tax in your listing prices and remit it yourself. Registration becomes required once you pass C$30,000 in sales over four consecutive quarters.

What Etsy never handles

Income tax. Your profit from Etsy — revenue after fees, materials, shipping, packaging and other deductible costs — is taxable income, and Etsy withholds nothing toward it. In the US, self-employed sellers generally pay federal income tax, state income tax where it applies, and the 15.3% self-employment tax on net earnings. A common starting point is to set aside 25–30% of profit as you go; an accountant who knows your state can tighten that number.

This is also why pricing matters so much: a 14.6% fee bill on a German order stings, but it is before you reserve anything for income tax. The pricing guide builds that reservation into the margin you should aim for.

The 1099-K, plainly

If you sell through Etsy Payments in the US, Etsy issues a Form 1099-K when your gross sales pass the reporting threshold. The federal threshold has been phasing down: $5,000 for 2024, $2,500 for 2025, and $600 from 2026 — and several states set their own lower ones. Download it from Shop Manager when it is available.

A 1099-K reports your gross transactions to the IRS — it is not a bill for tax on that whole amount. You owe income tax on your profit. Fees, postage, materials and equipment are all deductible, so keep the CSVs Etsy lets you download; they are your evidence.

What to actually set up

  • Add your tax ID (SSN/EIN, VAT number, or the local equivalent) under Shop Manager → Finances → Legal and financial information — it changes how tax is applied to your account.
  • Assign the correct category to every listing — Etsy uses it to pick the right tax rate for some items and jurisdictions.
  • Keep your shop's legal address current — tax on your fees and your filing obligations follow it.
  • Download the monthly CSV and keep it — it itemises sales, fees and tax collected, which is the record your tax return will be built from.
  • Set aside a fixed share of every payout for income tax, before you spend it.
Where these numbers come from. Etsy's own Taxes 101 seller handbook and its US sales tax help article cover what Etsy collects, and the IRS Gig Economy Tax Center and Form 1099-K pages cover what you owe. This page is general information, not tax advice — thresholds and state rules change, so verify against those sources or ask an accountant.
