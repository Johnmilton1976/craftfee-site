Etsy fees for EU sellers — one processing rate, six different VAT rates

Etsy charges a eurozone shop exactly the same payment processing as any other eurozone shop. What changes as you cross a border is tax — and, in three countries, an extra percentage fee that most fee calculators do not know about.

Everything else is the same as everywhere in the world: $0.20 listing fee billed in US dollars and converted (about €0.18), 6.5% transaction fee on item + shipping + gift wrap, Offsite Ads at 15% or 12% capped at US$100 per order, and a 2.5% conversion fee only if your listing currency differs from your payout currency.

Changed on 22 June 2026. France's regulatory fee went from 0.47% to 1.14%, Italy's from 0.32% to 0.80%, Spain's from 0.72% to 0.88%. France now carries the highest regulatory fee of any large European market — more than twice the UK's. If your spreadsheet predates that date, your margins are wrong.

The part that actually bites: VAT

VAT is not a fee. It is tax the buyer pays, and if you are VAT-registered you collect it and pass it on. But it changes what your "price" means, and this is where new sellers get their numbers wrong.

European prices are normally shown to consumers including VAT. So a €30 listing does not put €30 into your business. At 20% VAT, one sixth of it — €5.00 — belongs to the tax authority from the moment the order is placed.

Read the "you keep" column and a pattern appears: the country with the highest VAT rate is not the country that keeps the least. Italy has the highest VAT of the five, but France keeps €0.31 less on the same €30 sale, because its regulatory fee is the largest in Europe. VAT and the regulatory fee pull in different directions, and only one of them is reclaimable.

The mistake to avoid. New sellers take €30, subtract the cost of materials, and call the remainder profit. That overstates profit by the whole VAT amount. Start from the tax-exclusive figure — €25.21 in Germany, €24.79 in the Netherlands — and the arithmetic behaves.

VAT is charged on Etsy's fees too

Etsy applies VAT to its own charges where the rules require it. That means the €3.63 of fees above can cost a VAT-registered seller a little more on the invoice, with the VAT element reclaimable as input tax. If you are not VAT-registered, the VAT on fees is a real cost that nobody refunds.

Registration thresholds differ from country to country and are far from uniform — some are a few tens of thousands of euros, some much higher, and a few member states are changing them. Rather than rely on a table that goes stale, check your own state's threshold and the EU's one-stop-shop (OSS) scheme, which is how cross-border B2C sales of goods within the EU are usually declared once you pass it.

If you ship into the EU from outside it

The EU operates an import scheme (IOSS) for consignments of goods valued up to €150, under which the VAT is collected at the point of sale rather than at the border. Etsy handles collection for sales made through it, which means the buyer is charged VAT at checkout and it never becomes your revenue. Above €150 the old import rules apply and the buyer generally pays on delivery — which, in practice, is the single most common reason a European buyer refuses a parcel.

Quick answers

Is payment processing really the same in every eurozone country?

Yes — 4% + €0.30, no matter which eurozone country your bank account is in, and no matter where the buyer is. Etsy's processing table does show two different rates in a few non-euro countries — Canada and Australia charge less on domestic orders than on orders from abroad — but inside the eurozone there is only one rate.

Does the regulatory fee replace VAT?

No. They are unrelated. VAT is a tax on the buyer that passes through you. The regulatory operating fee is a fee charged to you and never returned.

Do I need to be VAT-registered to sell on Etsy in the EU?

Only once you cross your own country's turnover threshold, though some sellers register voluntarily. Note that Etsy charges VAT on its fees to non-registered sellers too — registration changes whether you can reclaim it, not whether it is added.

How do I see all of this on my own numbers?

Use the fee calculator, choose your country from the list, and enter your VAT rate in the VAT field — it strips the VAT out of the revenue figure instead of treating it as income, and applies the correct regulatory fee for that country. Rates for the individual countries are broken out on the regulatory fee page, and the UK page covers the non-euro case.
